Distributed Minimization Under Strong Growth

We minimize the mean of differentiable convex functions on Rd, where d is a nonnegative integer. The mean has a Lipschitz gradient and satisfies quadratic growth away from its minimizer set. The average squared norm of the local gradients is bounded by a constant times the squared norm of the mean gradient. A given starting point lies within a prescribed radius of a minimizer. On a star network, each worker returns an unbiased stochastic local gradient with bounded variance using independent samples. By the elapsed-time deadline, the output must have expected squared distance to the minimizer set at most ε, for a given tolerance ε>0.

For n≥1 workers with local objectives fi:Rd→R, define F(x)=n−1∑i=1nfi(x). The problem is minx∈RdF(x). Write S=argminx∈RdF(x) and dist(x,S)=infz∈S∥x−z∥, using the Euclidean norm. The algorithm receives d, n, the measurable sample space Ω, the starting point x0∈Rd, the network parameters (hi)i=1n,ℓ,b, and the constants L,μ,ρ,R,σ,ε.

Our assumptions:

Assumption 1 (Local convexity and mean smoothness). Each fi is convex and differentiable on Rd. For every x,y∈Rd, ∥∇F(x)−∇F(y)∥≤L∥x−y∥.

Assumption 2 (Strong growth of local gradients). For ρ≥1 and every x∈Rd, 1n∑i=1n∥∇fi(x)∥2≤ρ∥∇F(x)∥2.

Assumption 3 (Quadratic growth and initial distance). For 0<μ≤L and R≥0, there exists x⋆∈S such that ∥x0−x⋆∥≤R and, for every x∈Rd, F(x)−F(x⋆)≥μ2dist(x,S)2.

Assumption 4 (Stochastic local gradients). Worker i has a jointly measurable map gi:Rd×Ω→Rd and a sequence of measurable samples (ξi,k)k≥0 that are independent with common probability law νi. The sequences are independent across workers, and the algorithm’s randomness is independent of the entire sample array. At any query point x∈Rd, the worker’s (k+1)st completed evaluation returns (x,gi(x,ξi,k)). For ξ∼νi, the vector gi(x,ξ) is Bochner integrable and its squared error is integrable. For every i and x, with σ≥0, Eξ∼νi[gi(x,ξ)]=∇fi(x),Eξ∼νi[∥gi(x,ξ)−∇fi(x)∥2]≤σ2.

A central node connects to each worker, and only the workers evaluate stochastic gradients. An evaluation at worker i takes hi>0 time. Each worker handles one evaluation at a time, ignores new evaluation requests while busy, and can abort a pending evaluation without consuming a sample. Messages travel in either direction along a star edge. A payload of m≥0 bits is transmitted with 64 trailing zero bits and takes ℓ+b(m+64) time, where ℓ≥0 is the latency and b≥0 is the time per bit. The recipient receives the sender’s identity and the transmitted bits. Each directed link carries one message at a time and ignores submissions while occupied. Distinct directed links can operate concurrently, including opposite directions on the same edge, and communication can overlap with computation.

Let T≥0 be the elapsed-time budget. Almost surely over the samples and the algorithm’s randomness, only finitely many events may occur for every deadline t∈[0,T]. At time T, the algorithm’s designated output node must produce a measurable point x¯∈Rd with integrable squared distance to S satisfying E[dist(x¯,S)2]≤ε.
