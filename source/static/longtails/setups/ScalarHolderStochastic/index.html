Scalar Hölder Stochastic Stationarity

We consider a differentiable, possibly nonconvex function f:R→R with a globally Hölder continuous derivative. A given point x0∈R has objective gap at most Δ>0 above a global lower bound. At x, the oracle returns an unbiased scalar estimate of f′(x) using a fresh independent sample; its error has uniformly bounded pth absolute moment for 1<p≤2, allowing heavy-tailed noise. For ε>0, the goal is a measurable output x¯∈R satisfying P(|f′(x¯)|>ε)≤1/3, where P is the joint law of the samples and the algorithm’s independent randomness.

The underlying optimization problem is infx∈Rf(x), with accuracy measured by stationarity. Fix a measurable sample space Ω and parameters H>0,0<ν≤1,Δ>0,σ≥0,1<p≤2,ε>0. The algorithm receives Ω, H, ν, Δ, σ, p, ε, and x0; the objective and sampling distribution are accessed through the oracle.

Our assumptions:

Assumption 1 (Hölder continuity). The function f is differentiable everywhere, and |f′(x)−f′(y)|≤H|x−y|ν,x,y∈R.

Assumption 2 (Lower bound and initial gap). There exists ℓ∈R such that ℓ≤f(x)for every x∈R,f(x0)−ℓ≤Δ.

Assumption 3 (Independent stochastic derivative oracle). There exist a probability measure μ on Ω and a jointly measurable function g:R×Ω→R. For each x∈R, both g(x,⋅) and |g(x,⋅)−f′(x)|p are integrable under μ, and Eξ∼μ[g(x,ξ)]=f′(x),Eξ∼μ[|g(x,ξ)−f′(x)|p]≤σp. At call k≥0, a query x∈R receives the scalar reply g(x,ξk). The samples (ξk)k≥0 are measurable, mutually independent, and each has law μ. The algorithm’s randomness is independent of the entire sample sequence.

Queries may be made at any point of R. After the allotted oracle calls, the output x¯ must be measurable and satisfy P(|f′(x¯)|>ε)≤13.

  • Scalar Holder Heavy Tail Stationarity · Lean proof

Differentiable f:R→R satisfies |f′(x)−f′(y)|≤H|x−y|ν and f≥flow with f(x0)−flow≤Δ. Here H,Δ,ε>0, 0<ν≤1, σ≥0, and 1<p≤2. A jointly measurable oracle g has integrable values and central pth moments, ∫g(x,ω)dμ=f′(x) and ∫|g(x,ω)−f′(x)|pdμ≤σp. Samples are IID with law μ under a probability measure P. Target: measurable X with P(|f′(X)|>ε)≤1/3.
