Distributed Minimization Under Strong Growth

We minimize the mean of n≥1 smooth convex functions on Rd, where d is a nonnegative integer. The mean has quadratic growth away from its minimizer set, and the average squared norm of the local gradients is bounded by a multiple of the squared norm of the mean gradient. A given starting point lies within a prescribed radius of a minimizer. On a star network, each worker returns an unbiased local stochastic gradient with bounded variance using an independent sample stream. Given ε>0, the goal is a measurable output whose expected squared Euclidean distance to the minimizer set is at most ε by an elapsed-time deadline.

Write fi:Rd→R for the objective at worker i∈{1,…,n} and define F(x)=n−1∑i=1nfi(x). The optimization problem is minx∈RdF(x). Let S=argminx∈RdF(x) and write dist(x,S)=infz∈S∥x−z∥, using the Euclidean norm throughout.

Our assumptions:

Assumption 1 (Local and mean smoothness). Each fi is convex and differentiable on Rd. With local constants Li≥0 and quadratic growth parameter μ>0, set L=max{μ,max1≤i≤nLi}. For all x,y∈Rd, ∥∇fi(x)−∇fi(y)∥≤Li∥x−y∥(1≤i≤n),∥∇F(x)−∇F(y)∥≤L∥x−y∥.

Assumption 2 (Strong growth of local gradients). For some ρ≥1 and every x∈Rd, 1n∑i=1n∥∇fi(x)∥2≤ρ∥∇F(x)∥2.

Assumption 3 (Quadratic growth and initial distance). For the given x0∈Rd and R≥0, there exists x⋆∈S such that ∥x0−x⋆∥≤R and F(x)−F(x⋆)≥μ2dist(x,S)2(x∈Rd).

Assumption 4 (Local stochastic gradients). On a common measurable sample space Ω, worker i has a stream of measurable, independent samples (ξi,k)k≥0 with common law νi. The streams are mutually independent, and the algorithm’s randomness is independent of the entire sample array. At its kth completed evaluation, counting from zero, worker i returns the query point x∈Rd and gi(x,ξi,k), where gi:Rd×Ω→Rd is jointly measurable. For every i and x, the gradient estimate is Bochner integrable and its squared error is integrable under νi. For a common σ≥0, they satisfy Eξ∼νi[gi(x,ξ)]=∇fi(x),Eξ∼νi[∥gi(x,ξ)−∇fi(x)∥2]≤σ2.

The star has one central communication node and n computing workers. Worker i takes hi>0 time per evaluation. It can abort a pending evaluation without consuming a sample and ignores new evaluation requests while busy. Messages travel in either direction between the center and a worker. A payload of m≥0 bits carries an additional 64 zero bits, so its transmission takes ℓ+b(m+64) time, with latency ℓ≥0 and time per bit b≥0. The recipient receives the sender’s identity and the transmitted bits. Each directed link carries one message at a time and ignores submissions while occupied. Distinct directed links can operate concurrently with one another and with worker computations.

The algorithm receives d,n,Ω with its measurable structure, (hi)i=1n,ℓ,b,(Li)i=1n,μ,ρ,R,σ,ε, and x0. For an elapsed-time budget T≥0, almost surely the protocol must finish processing events in finitely many steps at every deadline t∈[0,T]. At time T, the algorithm’s designated output node must produce a measurable x¯∈Rd with integrable squared distance to S satisfying E[dist(x¯,S)2]≤ε, where the expectation is over the algorithm’s randomness and the workers’ samples.

Results

  • Distributed Locally Smooth Growth · Lean proof

On Rd, each fi is convex with public gradient Lipschitz bound Li. The mean F=n−1∑ifi is L¯-smooth and satisfies F(x)−minF≥(μ/2)dist(x,X∗)2 and n−1∑i∥∇fi(x)∥2≤ρ∥∇F(x)∥2, with μ>0 and ρ≥1. A minimizer lies within R of x0. Jointly measurable worker gradients are unbiased with variance at most σ2, with IID samples within workers and independence across workers. In the finite-bit coordinator star, each worker handles one gradient at a time in hi>0 time and each directed link handles one packet at a time at latency ℓ and cost b per bit. Distinct workers and directed links can run concurrently. The target is Edist(X,X∗)2≤ε for the returned point.
