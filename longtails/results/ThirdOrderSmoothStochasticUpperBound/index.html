Third-Order Smooth Stochastic Nonconvex Optimization

We seek an approximate stationary point of a possibly nonconvex function f:E→R on a finite-dimensional real inner product space E. Its first three derivatives are globally Lipschitz, and the gap between the starting value and the infimum is bounded. At each query x∈E, the oracle uses a fresh independent sample to return an unbiased estimate of ∇f(x) with uniformly bounded variance. For a tolerance ε>0, the goal is a measurable output x¯∈E satisfying E[∥∇f(x¯)∥]≤ε, where the expectation includes both oracle and algorithm randomness.

The underlying minimization problem is infx∈Ef(x). The algorithm receives E with its Borel σ-algebra, the measurable sample space Ω, the starting point x0∈E, the smoothness constants L1,L2,L3>0, the noise bound σ≥0, the initial gap bound Δ≥0, and ε. We write ∥⋅∥ for the inner product norm and ∥⋅∥op for the induced operator and multilinear norms.

Our assumptions:

Assumption 1 (Smoothness through third order). The function f is three times continuously differentiable. For all x,y∈E, ∥∇f(x)−∇f(y)∥≤L1∥x−y∥,∥∇2f(x)−∇2f(y)∥op≤L2∥x−y∥,∥∇3f(x)−∇3f(y)∥op≤L3∥x−y∥.

Assumption 2 (Initial objective gap). The starting point satisfies f(x0)−infx∈Ef(x)≤Δ.

Assumption 3 (Stochastic gradient oracle). On call k at any query x∈E, the oracle returns g(x,ξk), where g:E×Ω→E is jointly measurable. The samples (ξk)k≥0 are independent random variables with common law μ on Ω, and the sample sequence is independent of the algorithm’s randomness. For every fixed x∈E and ξ∼μ, the vector g(x,ξ) is Bochner integrable, the squared error ∥g(x,ξ)−∇f(x)∥2 is integrable, and Eξ∼μ[g(x,ξ)]=∇f(x),Eξ∼μ[∥g(x,ξ)−∇f(x)∥2]≤σ2.

After the allotted oracle calls, the output x¯ must be measurable under the joint randomness of the algorithm and the sample sequence. Its gradient norm must be integrable under this law and satisfy E[∥∇f(x¯)∥]≤ε.
