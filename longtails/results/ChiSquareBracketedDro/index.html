Chi-Square Constrained Robust Optimization

We minimize a robust objective F over a closed convex set C in a separable real Hilbert space E. Here F(x) is the worst expected sample loss under nonnegative densities relative to a probability law ν, with mean one and chi-square divergence at most ρ>0. Sample losses are jointly measurable, convex and bounded on C, with jointly measurable bounded subgradients. Each oracle call returns the projection of the query onto C, together with its loss and subgradient evaluated using one fresh independent sample from ν. Given ε>0, the goal is a measurable feasible output x¯ with E[F(x¯)−F(x⋆)]≤ε, where x⋆ minimizes F on C and expectation includes sampling and algorithm randomness.

Let Ξ be a measurable sample space carrying the probability measure ν. Write ℓ:E×Ξ→R for the sample loss and g:E×Ξ→E for its supplied subgradient. Define the density class Wρ={w:Ξ→R measurable:w(ξ)≥0 for every ξ,w∈L2(ν),Eξ∼ν[w(ξ)]=1,12Eξ∼ν[(w(ξ)−1)2]≤ρ}. The objective and optimization problem are F(x)=supw∈WρEξ∼ν[w(ξ)ℓ(x,ξ)],minx∈CF(x). The norm ∥⋅∥ is induced by the inner product on E. The algorithm receives E, Ξ, ρ>0, G>0, B>0, R≥0, ε>0, and a given point x0∈E; the instance consists of C, ν, ℓ, and g, accessed through the oracle described below.

Our assumptions:

Assumption 1 (Feasible set and projection). The set C⊆E is closed and convex, and x0∈C. There is a measurable map ΠC:E→C satisfying ⟨y−ΠC(y),u−ΠC(y)⟩≤0,y∈E,u∈C.

Assumption 2 (Sample losses and subgradients). The maps ℓ and g are jointly measurable. For every ξ∈Ξ and x,y∈C, ℓ(x,ξ)+⟨g(x,ξ),y−x⟩≤ℓ(y,ξ),0≤ℓ(x,ξ)≤B,∥g(x,ξ)∥≤G.

Assumption 3 (Initial distance). There exists x⋆∈C such that F(x⋆)≤F(x)for every x∈C,∥x0−x⋆∥≤R.

Queries may be made at any y∈E. At call k≥0, the oracle returns (ΠC(y), ℓ(ΠC(y),ξk), g(ΠC(y),ξk)). The sample variables (ξk)k≥0 are measurable, mutually independent, and each has law ν. The loss and subgradient in a reply use the same sample. Any algorithm randomness is independent of the entire sample sequence.

After the allotted calls, the output x¯ must be measurable and belong to C for every realization. Its objective gap must be integrable and satisfy EP[F(x¯)−F(x⋆)]≤ε, where P is the joint law of the samples and the algorithm’s randomness.
