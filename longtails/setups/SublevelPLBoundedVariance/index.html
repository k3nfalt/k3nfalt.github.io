Sublevel Gradient Domination with Stochastic Gradients

We consider minimizing a possibly nonconvex differentiable function f:E→R on a finite-dimensional real inner product space E. Its gradient is L-Lipschitz, with L>0, and f⋆=infx∈Ef(x) is finite. Given x0∈E, assume f(x)−f⋆≤τ∥∇f(x)∥α whenever f(x)≤f(x0), where τ>0 and 1≤α<2. The oracle returns an unbiased stochastic gradient with variance at most σ2, where σ≥0, using independent, identically distributed samples. For ε>0, the goal is an output x¯∈E with integrable objective gap and EP[f(x¯)−f⋆]≤ε, where P is the joint law of algorithm and oracle randomness.

The optimization problem is infx∈Ef(x). The norm ∥⋅∥ is induced by the inner product on E. The algorithm receives E, L, τ, α, σ, an initial-gap bound Δ≥0, the tolerance ε, and the given point x0.

Our assumptions:

Assumption 1 (Smoothness). The function f is Fréchet differentiable everywhere on E, and ∥∇f(x)−∇f(y)∥≤L∥x−y∥,x,y∈E.

Assumption 2 (Sublevel gradient domination). The function f is bounded below, and its initial sublevel set satisfies f(x)−f⋆≤τ∥∇f(x)∥αwhenever f(x)≤f(x0),f(x0)−f⋆≤Δ.

Assumption 3 (Unbiased gradients with bounded variance). Let Ω be a measurable sample space and ν a probability measure on Ω. There is a jointly measurable map g:E×Ω→E such that, for every fixed x∈E, g(x,⋅) is Bochner integrable and ∥g(x,⋅)−∇f(x)∥2 is integrable under ν, with Eξ∼ν[g(x,ξ)]=∇f(x),Eξ∼ν[∥g(x,ξ)−∇f(x)∥2]≤σ2. The samples (ξk)k≥0 have a joint probability law Q on ΩN. Each coordinate is measurable, the coordinates are mutually independent, and each has distribution ν. At query xk∈E, the oracle returns g(xk,ξk). The algorithm’s randomness is independent of this sample sequence.

Queries may be made at any point of E. After the allotted oracle calls, the output must have an integrable objective gap under P and satisfy EP[f(x¯)−f⋆]≤ε.

On a finite-dimensional real inner-product space, f is differentiable, globally L-smooth and bounded below. With f⋆=infxf(x), assume f(x0)−f⋆≤Δ and f(x)−f⋆≤τ∥∇f(x)∥α whenever f(x)≤f(x0), where L,τ>0, 1≤α<2, σ,Δ≥0 and ε>0. The jointly measurable oracle uses IID samples from a probability measure p, with ∫g(x,z)dp(z)=∇f(x) and ∫∥g(x,z)−∇f(x)∥2dp(z)≤σ2; these integrals are integrable.
