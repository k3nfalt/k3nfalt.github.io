High-Probability Stationarity in Stochastic Optimization

We seek an approximately stationary point of a possibly nonconvex function f:E→R on a separable real Hilbert space E. Its gradient is Lipschitz, and the objective gap at a given starting point is bounded relative to the finite infimum of f. At each query, the oracle uses a fresh independent sample from a common distribution to return an unbiased gradient estimate with uniformly bounded variance. For tolerance ε>0 and failure probability 0<δ≤1/4, the goal is a measurable output x¯∈E satisfying ∥∇f(x¯)∥≤ε with probability at least 1−δ.

The underlying optimization problem is infx∈Ef(x). Equip E with its Borel σ-algebra and write ∥⋅∥ for its Hilbert norm. The algorithm receives E, the measurable sample space Ω, the starting point x0∈E, the smoothness constant L>0, the noise bound σ≥0, the initial gap bound Δ≥0, and the accuracy parameters ε and δ.

Our assumptions:

Assumption 1 (Smoothness and initial gap). The function f is Fréchet differentiable on E, and its gradient satisfies ∥∇f(x)−∇f(y)∥≤L∥x−y∥,x,y∈E. The infimum f⋆=infx∈Ef(x) is finite and satisfies f(x0)−f⋆≤Δ.

Assumption 2 (Stochastic gradient oracle). On call k, a query at any x∈E returns g(x,ξk), where g:E×Ω→E is jointly measurable. The samples (ξk)k≥0 are independent random variables with common law ν on Ω, and the sample sequence is independent of the algorithm’s randomness. For every x∈E and ξ∼ν, the estimate g(x,ξ) is Bochner integrable, its squared error ∥g(x,ξ)−∇f(x)∥2 is integrable, and Eξ∼ν[g(x,ξ)]=∇f(x),Eξ∼ν[∥g(x,ξ)−∇f(x)∥2]≤σ2.

After the allotted oracle calls, the output x¯ must be measurable and satisfy P(∥∇f(x¯)∥≤ε)≥1−δ, where P is the joint law of the algorithm’s randomness and the oracle samples.

  • High Probability Stationarity Upper Bound · Lean proof

Let f be differentiable and bounded below on a complete, second-countable real inner-product space with its Borel σ-algebra. Assume ∇f is L-Lipschitz and f(x0)−inff≤Δ. The jointly measurable stochastic-gradient oracle uses IID samples, with mean ∇f(x) and mean squared error at most σ2 at each x. The target is one measurable point x^ satisfying Pr(∥∇f(x^)∥≤ε)≥1−δ, where L,ε>0, Δ,σ≥0, and 0<δ≤1/4.
