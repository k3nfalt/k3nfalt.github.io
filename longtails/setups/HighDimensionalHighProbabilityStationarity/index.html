High-Dimensional Stationarity with High Probability

We seek an approximately stationary point of a possibly nonconvex function f:Rd→R in high dimension d. Its gradient is L-Lipschitz for L>0, and the objective value at a given starting point x0 is within Δ of the finite infimum. At each query, the oracle uses a fresh independent sample to return an unbiased gradient estimate with mean squared error at most σ2, where σ≥0. Given a tolerance ε>0 and a failure probability 0<δ≤1/4, the goal is a measurable output x¯∈Rd satisfying ∥∇f(x¯)∥≤ε with probability at least 1−δ.

Write f⋆ for the value of the optimization problem infx∈Rdf(x), and use the Euclidean norm throughout. The algorithm receives d, L, σ, Δ, ε, δ, and x0. The gap bound Δ and the dimension d∈N lie in the regime LΔ≥254ε2,d≥240(1+LΔε2(1+σ2ε2))4.

Our assumptions:

Assumption 1 (Smoothness and initial gap). The function f is differentiable on Rd, and for all x,y∈Rd, ∥∇f(x)−∇f(y)∥≤L∥x−y∥. Its infimum f⋆ is finite and satisfies f(x0)−f⋆≤Δ.

Assumption 2 (Stochastic gradient oracle). On call k at any x∈Rd, the oracle returns g(x,ξk), where g:Rd×R→Rd is jointly measurable. The real-valued samples (ξk)k≥0 are independent with common law ν, and the sequence is independent of the algorithm’s randomness. For each x∈Rd and ξ∼ν, the vector g(x,ξ) is Bochner integrable, the squared error ∥g(x,ξ)−∇f(x)∥2 is integrable, and Eξ∼ν[g(x,ξ)]=∇f(x),Eξ∼ν[∥g(x,ξ)−∇f(x)∥2]≤σ2.

After the allotted oracle calls, the output x¯ must be measurable and satisfy P(∥∇f(x¯)∥≤ε)≥1−δ, where P is the joint law of the algorithm’s randomness and the oracle samples.

Results

  • High Probability Stationarity Lower Bound · Lean proof

Let f:Rd→R have an L-Lipschitz gradient and f(x0)−inff≤Δ, with L>0 and ε>0. The jointly measurable stochastic gradient uses IID real seeds, is unbiased, and has variance at most σ2 (σ≥0). The target for a measurable output x^ is Pr(∥∇f(x^)∥≤ε)≥1−δ. The setup requires 0<δ≤1/4, LΔ≥254ε2, and d≥240(1+B)4.
