Generalized Lipschitz Monotone Root Finding

We seek a zero of a continuous monotone operator F:E→E on a real inner product space E with induced norm ∥⋅∥. For constants L0>0 and L1≥0, the Lipschitz bound along each segment is L0+L1M whenever M bounds the norm of F on that segment. A given point x0∈E satisfies ∥F(x0)∥≤G and lies within distance R of a zero, where G,R≥0. The oracle returns F(x) at x. For ε>0, the goal is an output x¯∈E with ∥F(x¯)∥≤ε almost surely under P, the law of the algorithm’s randomness.

The root-finding problem is to find x∈E such that F(x)=0. Write ⟨⋅,⋅⟩ for the inner product on E. The algorithm receives only E, L0, L1, and x0; the residual bound G, radius R, and tolerance ε may enter the oracle-call budget but are not algorithm inputs.

Our assumptions:

Assumption 1 (Continuity and monotonicity). The operator F is continuous, and for every x,y∈E, ⟨F(x)−F(y),x−y⟩≥0.

Assumption 2 (Generalized Lipschitz condition). For every x,y∈E and M∈R satisfying ∥F(x+θ(y−x))∥≤Mfor every θ∈[0,1], the operator satisfies ∥F(x)−F(y)∥≤(L0+L1M)∥x−y∥.

Assumption 3 (Initial residual and distance). There exists x⋆∈E such that F(x⋆)=0,∥x0−x⋆∥≤R,∥F(x0)∥≤G.

Queries may be made at any point of E. After the allotted oracle calls, the output must satisfy the residual criterion ∥F(x¯)∥≤εP-a.s.

  • Anchored Generalized Monotone · Lean proof

A continuous monotone map F:E→E on a real inner-product space satisfies ∥F(x)−F(y)∥≤(L0+L1max0≤t≤1∥F(x+t(y−x))∥)∥x−y∥. Here L0>0, L1,G,R≥0, ε>0, ∥F(x0)∥≤G, and some root x⋆ satisfies ∥x0−x⋆∥≤R. The oracle returns exact F(x); the target is ∥F(x)∥≤ε deterministically. The setup imposes no algorithm restriction.
